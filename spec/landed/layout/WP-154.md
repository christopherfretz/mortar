# WP-154: `-r` (relocatable output), the design

This is a design record; no linker code was written. The rules are tagged
M4-r in their owning specs:

- `spec/cli.md` §3.2 (corrected) and §3.16 C-R1 to C-R6;
- `spec/resolve.md` §11 RV-R1 to RV-R10;
- `spec/layout.md` §24 L-R1 to L-R10 (and L-103);
- `spec/image.md` §17 I-R1 to I-R12 (and I-11);
- `spec/arch.md` §4.6 A-R1 to A-R5 (and §3.3);
- `spec/model.md` §4.6 MD-R1 to MD-R3;
- `spec/meta.md` MT-30 (checked), MT-30a, MT-16.

The verifier's side is a list of numbered requests (§D) and is not an
edit of `spec/plumb.md`. The experiments are in
`tests/relocatable-design/` (the README there lists them; `exp/run-all.sh
WORK` reruns all of them). This file cites them by their path in that
directory.

Environment: AArch64 host, 12 cores shared. GCC/G++/gfortran 14.2.0
(native, and the x86_64 and i686 cross compilers); clang 19.1.7 and
22.1.8; GNU ld 2.44 and objcopy 2.44; ld.lld 22.1.8; mold 2.37.1;
qemu-user 10.0.13; glibc 2.41; gdb 16; lldb 19. mortar is the binary
`mortar-3ad9867a` (batch 10's tree, a debug build). kiln++ is kiln
d854b23bc (`.build/target-kiln-next`, read-only), and hod is the
workspace's release build.

## A. Census

### A.1 BusyBox (`exp/busybox/`, `work/busybox.log`)

I built BusyBox 1.36.1 (defconfig, `CONFIG_TC` off) by hand with `make
-j4 V=1`, with GCC and with clang-22. Its Kbuild has `LD = $(CC)
-nostdlib` and `cmd_link_o_target = $(LD) -nostdlib $(ld_flags) -r -o
$@ $(filter $(obj-y), $^)`. A directory with nothing to link gets an
empty archive named `built-in.o` (`ar rcs`), not a `-r`. The survey's
two lines are the `-r` of `applets/applets.o` into
`applets/built-in.o`, once per compiler:

- gcc: `-plugin …/liblto_plugin.so -plugin-opt=…/lto-wrapper
  -plugin-opt=-fresolution=… --eh-frame-hdr --hash-style=gnu
  --as-needed -dynamic-linker /lib/ld-linux-aarch64.so.1 -X -EL
  -maarch64linux --fix-cortex-a53-843419 -o applets/built-in.o -r
  applets/applets.o`
- clang-22: `-EL --hash-style=gnu --build-id --eh-frame-hdr -m
  aarch64linux -o applets/built-in.o -r applets/applets.o`

GNU ld, lld and mold all link both lines. Their outputs, replayed into
BusyBox's final `trylink` line (`--sort-common --sort-section alignment
--gc-sections --start-group … --end-group`), give the same program from
GNU ld and from mortar (`ok|42|edeaaff3f1774ad2`, the applet's output
hash). mortar refuses the `-r` itself: `unsupported option: -r`.

### A.3 Lines that are not from a driver (`exp/facts/`, `exp/options/`, `exp/more/`)

- **Kernel-style** `ld -r -o built-in.o a.o b.o`, with and without
  `-m`: every reference links it.
- **`-T script`**: GNU ld and lld honour `SECTIONS` at `-r`, so
  `.text.*` goes into `.text`. mold refuses the script. The final links
  run (`more.log`, `script-*`).
- **`--whole-archive` of a thin archive**, `--start-group`: all three.
- **`ld -r -b binary -o blob.o file`**: GNU ld writes `.data` (WA,
  alignment 1) and `_binary_*_start`, `_end`, `_size` (ABS). lld needs
  `-m` and gives alignment 8. mold refuses `-b binary`.
  `--format=binary` with `-b default` back to objects: GNU ld and lld.
- **`-Ur`**: GNU ld writes the same sections as for `-r`; lld and mold
  reject it. **`-i`**: GNU ld writes the same bytes as `-r`; lld and
  mold reject it (`more.log`).

### A.4 The pre-link idiom (`exp/more/run.sh`, `exp/more/prelink.sh`)

The idiom is `ld -r` of a component, then `objcopy --localize-hidden`
(or `-G`), then a final link.

- **C**: it works with GNU ld's and lld's `-r`. The hidden symbol `hid`
  is `GLOBAL HIDDEN` after `-r` and `LOCAL HIDDEN` after
  `--localize-hidden`. The final links by GNU ld and by mortar run like
  the direct link. With mold's `-r` the program crashes, from mold's
  COMMON bug (§D).
- **C++ with exceptions, plain `-r`**: it aborts. This happens with every
  reference's `-r` and with GNU ld's and mortar's final links alike:
  `terminate called after throwing an instance of 'int'`. The mechanism
  (`prelink.log`):
  - after `-r`, the component's only hidden globals are the signatures
    of two COMDAT groups, `DW.ref.__gxx_personality_v0` and
    `DW.ref._ZTISt13runtime_error`;
  - `--localize-hidden` makes them local, and the groups stay groups;
  - in the final link, `mainx.o`'s group of the same signature loses the
    election, so its `.eh_frame` CIE names a global whose only
    definition is in the dropped member;
  - the reference resolves as an undefined weak, value 0, and the
    personality pointer is null.
  This was reported to the supervisor at once. It is a hole in
  `spec/resolve.md` §6.2/§6.3 that is wider than `-r`: the supervisor
  reproduced it with two hand-written objects and no `-r` or objcopy.
  The ruling is that a reference through a demoted mention whose key
  ends with no definition is `comdat-mismatch` whatever the mention's
  binding. That fix is **WP-176**, a linker package, plus a verifier
  package, and is not part of this design.
- **What makes the idiom sound with C++.** `--localize-hidden` may
  localize a symbol only when no group carries it. A group's signature
  and its member symbols must stay as they are, or the group must be
  dissolved first. objcopy 2.44 does not check this, and `ld -r` by the
  three references keeps every COMDAT group (B.2), so the hidden
  `DW.ref.*` symbols are group signatures. The sound form is `ld -r
  --force-group-allocation` (GNU ld, lld) before localizing: no group is
  left (0 groups, the 2 hidden symbols outside any group), the
  component uses its own local `DW.ref` copies, and `mainx.o` keeps its
  group. Measured with EH and `-g` at `-O0`:

  | `-r` by | final GNU ld | final mortar |
  |---|---|---|
  | GNU ld | aborts (gdb transcript same) | refused (finding 1, §D) |
  | GNU ld `--force-group-allocation` | **runs, gdb same** | refused (finding 1) |
  | lld | aborts | aborts |
  | lld `--force-group-allocation` | **runs, gdb same** | **runs, gdb same** |
  | mold | aborts | aborts |

  mortar's `-r` offers the sound form: `--force-group-allocation` is
  part of RL-8 (`spec/cli.md` C-R3, `spec/resolve.md` RV-R7). A
  dissolved group costs duplicate code when the final link also has a
  copy, because both copies are linked in and every reference binds to
  the first global definition. Weak globals that are not hidden keep one
  identity.

### B.4 Relocations

Every record type is carried unchanged by all three. There is no
relaxation, no GOT, no PLT, no TLS transition and no range check
(`facts.log` row 6, `facts-supp.log` 9-gotpcrelx). The types seen:
- x86-64: `GOTTPOFF`, `PC32`, `PLT32`, `REX_GOTPCRELX`, `TLSGD`;
- AArch64: `ADR_GOT_PAGE`, `LD64_GOT_LO12_NC`, `CALL26`, `PREL32`,
  `TLSDESC_ADR_PAGE21`, `TLSDESC_LD64_LO12`, `TLSDESC_ADD_LO12`,
  `TLSDESC_CALL`;
- i386: `GOT32X`, `GOTPC`, `PC32`, `PLT32`, `TLS_GOTIE`.
On i386 (REL) the addend of a section-symbol record is rewritten in the
bytes by all three (`8-rel-addend`: `.data` holds `0b 16`, and rcheck
finds "records equal 2"). Against the C++ component (rcheck,
`final/<arch>.gcc/comp.cxx.*.o`, `work/rcheck-cxx.log`):
- GNU ld: every input record has its output record, equal (AArch64
  2264 of 2264; x86-64 2230; i686 2044). The only output records
  rcheck cannot explain are its `.eh_frame` `NONE` records.
- lld and mold: records into merged strings name the same string
  (1640, 1617, 1613); the rest are equal.
- lld on i686 has one record missing: the dropped `get_pc_thunk` FDE's.
Nested `-r` (a `-r` output as the input of a `-r`, with a group whose
signature the other input repeats): the same group and section counts as
the flat `-r`, in all three.

### B.5 `.eh_frame`, mergeable and debug sections

- **`.eh_frame`**: see B.2. No driver adds `crtend.o` to a `-r` line
  unless `-no-pie -Wl,-r` without `-nostdlib` (A.2).
- **Mergeable sections**: GNU ld does not merge them. lld and mold merge
  `.debug_str`, `.debug_line_str`, `.comment` and `.rodata.*.str*`, and
  the records into them are correct. Nothing breaks either way in the
  corpus.
- **Debug at `-O0 -g`, C++, AArch64** (`work/debug.log`): gdb stops at
  `x1.cc:10` and `x2.cc:8` with the same backtraces through both inputs
  on every route that links:
  - bfd→bfd, lld→bfd, mold→bfd, lld→mortar and mold→mortar equal the
    direct link;
  - bfd→mortar is refused (finding 1).
  lldb gives the same transcripts on every route, compared against the
  final linker's own direct link. mortar's direct link and its `-r`
  routes differ from GNU ld's only in the libc frame's alias name
  (`__libc_start_main` against `__libc_start_main_impl`), which is not
  specific to `-r`.
- **`-gsplit-dwarf`**: every route links and runs. The skeleton-unit
  count was not read (Appendix B).
- **`--gdb-index` at `-r`**: lld refuses it, mold writes nothing.
- **Fortran** (a module with `bind(c)` and a module variable): every
  route runs, `11`.

### B.6 TLS, IFUNC, PIC

TLS records pass through unchanged (B.4). IFUNC: mold crashes on the
definition; GNU ld and lld keep `STT_GNU_IFUNC`. `-fPIC`, `-fPIE` and
non-PIC objects in one `-r`: nothing in the output depends on the code
model. LTO: B.3.

### B.7 The file

All three write `ET_REL`, `e_phnum` 0, `e_entry` 0 (without `-e`),
`e_flags` 0 and mode 644, and give identical bytes for the same line
twice (`facts.log` row 7). `EI_OSABI` is `GNU` for an input with
`STB_GNU_UNIQUE`.

### B.8 `.mason.meta` (`exp/meta/`, `work/meta.log`)

kiln++ at `-fmason-metadata=detail`, by both the llc route and the hod
route, writes one container per object: `PROGBITS`, `SHF_EXCLUDE`,
alignment 8, one unit (kinds 1, 3, 4, 5), no group, no relocation
section. A `-r` by each reference makes one container of three units,
in input order, unchanged. On the direct route and through each
reference's `-r`, `--odr=strict` gives the same `type 1S violates the
one-definition rule`, with the same source lines. Through a `-r` the
object names (`r.bfd.o`, twice) no longer tell the two units apart; the
`source=` lines still do. The legal set runs on every route without a
diagnostic. MT-30 holds. MT-16's section anchors do not survive a merge
of same-named sections (`spec/meta.md` MT-16, Q10). The ODR checks also
run at `-r` (MT-30a, Q11).

## D. The verifier's side

### D.1 Requests (for `spec/plumb.md`'s owners; not applied here)

- **V-R1 (the oracle is the final program).** A `-r` is judged by
  composition: stage 3 judges the final link of the `-r` output against
  its inputs, and V-R2-V-R8 judge the `-r` output against the `-r`'s
  inputs. The lane adds the end-to-end comparison. With the component's
  objects contiguous on the direct line and the `-r` output in their
  place, `link(inputs)` and `link(-r output)` must give:
  - the same output sections, in the same order;
  - per input piece, the same bytes after relocation;
  - the same symbol values for every global.
  What MAY differ, and nothing else:
  - (a) addresses, by the padding that a merged section's alignment
    adds at its start: the `-r`'s `.text` is one input aligned to its
    largest piece;
  - (b) `.symtab`'s local entries: the `-r` output's `STT_SECTION` and
    `STT_FILE` symbols, and the order of locals;
  - (c) the build ID, which hashes (a) and (b);
  - (d) the COMDAT keeper of a group whose copies' bytes differ and
    which has a copy outside the component (the `-r` elects first).
  The program is also run, both ways round.
- **V-R2 (every live input section once).** Each input section of a live
  file that RV-R7/I-R9 do not drop is exactly one piece of one output
  section of L-R1's name. It sits at an offset aligned to its
  `sh_addralign`, and its bytes equal the input's outside relocated
  fields. On REL a relocated field holds `A + delta`. Mergeable pieces
  are byte-identical (L-R5). Dropped sections are absent.
- **V-R3 (every input relocation once).** For each input record that is
  not `NONE` and not dropped by RV-R7, there is exactly one output record
  at the moved offset, with the same type and an equivalent target. A
  global is equivalent when name and addend are the same. A local or
  section target is equivalent when it is the same (output section,
  offset), with the addend in the record (RELA) or in the field (REL).
  There are no other records: no `NONE`. This is rcheck's rule;
  `exp/tools/rcheck.py` is the prototype, minus its `.eh_frame` limit
  (V-R5).
- **V-R4 (the symbol table).** These must hold:
  - locals first and `sh_info` correct;
  - the `STT_SECTION` set and order of I-R4;
  - every key of RV-R6 exactly once, with the binding, type and
    visibility the rules give: hidden stays global, `UNIQUE` kept,
    commons per RV-R5, undefined per RV-R3, versioned names unchanged;
  - no linker-defined symbol (RV-R8);
  - the signatures of the groups present.
- **V-R5 (`.eh_frame` parsed).** CIE/FDE framing valid; each kept FDE's
  CIE pointer names a CIE of its input; the FDEs of dropped sections are
  absent; every other input FDE is present once, byte for byte except
  its CIE pointer; its records are moved by V-R3.
- **V-R6 (groups).** One `SHT_GROUP` per kept group, with the signature
  symbol, `GRP_COMDAT` and the members (with their relocation sections)
  `SHF_GROUP`. No member is in two groups. The keepers are recomputed
  by `spec/resolve.md` §6.1 over the `-r`'s inputs alone. Non-COMDAT
  groups are all present. With `--force-group-allocation` there is no
  group.
- **V-R7 (the header).** `ET_REL`; no program headers; `e_entry` 0; I-R2's
  `EI_OSABI`; A-R3's `e_flags`; L-R8's section order; I-R3's mode.
- **V-R8 (the Mason container).** At most one container. Its bytes equal
  `kept_units` over the live inputs (MT-30), and no input container
  survives as a section.
- **V-R9 (S1 on `ET_REL`).** `spec/harness.md` says that `relocatable` is
  not judged today. The request is that S1 judge it with V-R2-V-R8.
- **V-R10 (mutations).** At least one per rule, each caught:
  - a record moved by 4 bytes;
  - a section-symbol addend without the piece offset (RELA, and REL in
    the field);
  - a piece dropped or written twice;
  - two copies of a COMDAT group kept;
  - a hidden global demoted to local;
  - a common turned `SHN_ABS` (mold's bug);
  - a `.symver` name stripped;
  - an FDE of a dropped copy kept;
  - a `NONE` record left at a live record's offset;
  - a container duplicated.

## E. Incremental and speed

**Incremental (IN-12).** A pre-linked component should be **ignored** as
a unit by the incremental design, and treated as one ordinary input
object: when it changes, its pieces are compared like any input's. Two
choices of this design make that cheap later, so nothing more is needed
now:
- mortar's `-r` keeps every input piece byte-identical (L-R5: no
  merging; L-R6: `.eh_frame` records copied), so a content-hash diff at
  piece level finds the unchanged pieces of a re-pre-linked component;
- the `-r` layout is data (MD-R1), so a later "incremental `-r`" can
  move pieces without new kinds of state.
A `-r` output loses per-object identity (one `FileId`), which costs the
incremental design's per-file granularity. That is a reason not to
build on `-r` outputs, not a thing to fix in `-r`. IN-12 stays:
`--incremental` with `-r` is a warning and a plain link.

**Speed** (`exp/bench/`, `work/bench.log`). The input was the first 400
objects under `lib/` of `.build/bench-work`'s cxx-large-g tree
(read-only): 326 MB, `-g`. Three runs each, on the shared host:

| reference | wall time | peak RSS | output | sections | symbols | `.rela*` | `.strtab` | groups |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GNU ld | 8.5-11.5 s | 225 MB | 322 MB | 48,879 | 86,048 | 67.1 MB in 16,323 sections (2.79 M records) | 4.65 MB | 10,434 |
| lld 22 | 0.89-1.16 s | 770 MB | 245 MB | 48,879 | 86,047 | 67.1 MB | 6.48 MB | 10,434 |
| mold | 1.17-1.31 s | 931 MB | 245 MB | 48,879 | 101,978 | 66.9 MB | 6.48 MB | 10,434 |

What `-r` costs a large link:
- **Relocations dominate.** 2.8 M records for 400 objects, about 7k per
  object, so about 16 M for the whole tree's 2,375 objects. Each is read,
  moved and renamed once (I-R5), with no scan.
- **Merged strings: 77 MB here.** GNU ld's output is 77 MB larger
  because it does not merge `.debug_str` and friends. L-R5 accepts that
  cost (Q2).
- **`.strtab`.** GNU ld deduplicates symbol names (4.65 MB against
  6.48 MB).
- **Memory.** lld and mold hold 2.4 and 2.9 times the input in memory.
  GNU ld streams, using 0.7 times the input, at ten times their time.
  mortar's `-r` should stay below the input's size: pieces are copied by
  positioned writes and relocation sections are rewritten per input
  section in parallel (`spec/image.md` §10's design). That is a target
  for the bench, not a package of its own (order of work: no package for
  speed alone).

## G. Open questions (each with its recommendation)

- **Q1 (closed by this design): `-Ur` and `-i`.** Accept both, as
  `-r`. GNU ld links them; a line GNU ld links must link.
- **Q2 (merging at `-r`).** GNU ld does not merge; lld and mold do.
  *Recommendation:* do not merge (L-R5). The verifier matches bytes,
  the final link merges anyway, and pieces stay reusable. Revisit only
  if file size matters on a real line (77 MB of 322 here).
- **Q3 (section order).** The three references differ. *Recommendation:*
  first appearance, with each relocation section after its target
  (L-R8). It is a function of input order alone, so V-R7 recomputes it
  without a name table.
- **Q4 (`.eh_frame` of a dropped copy).** GNU ld leaves `NONE` records
  that break mortar's own final link (finding 1); lld keeps a dangling
  FDE; mold rewrites everything. *Recommendation:* remove the FDE and
  its records, keep CIEs, write no `NONE` (L-R6). Every byte left is an
  input byte, and V-R5 can check it.
- **Q5 (debug records into a dropped copy).** The corpus had none.
  *Recommendation:* no record and the final tombstone in the field
  (I-R7). RL-8 first measures GNU ld's `-r` on a case that has one
  (`-O0 -g` inline functions with `-fno-eliminate-unused-debug-types`,
  or `-gdwarf-4` `DW_AT_ranges`). If GNU ld writes a record instead,
  this rule changes before RL-8 implements it.
- **Q6 (`e_entry` with `-e`).** GNU ld and lld write an offset, mold 0.
  *Recommendation:* 0 (L-R7). An `ET_REL` entry means nothing, and 0 is
  verifiable.
- **Q7 (crt files in a `-r`).** `-no-pie -Wl,-r` without `-nostdlib`
  pre-links `crt1.o … crtend.o`, which puts an `.eh_frame` terminator
  into the middle of the final link's `.eh_frame`. *Recommendation:*
  link it, because GNU ld does, but warn by name when a `-r` input
  defines `_start` or holds a zero-length `.eh_frame` terminator that is
  not at the end of its input. The warning is part of RL-11.
- **Q8 (an `SHT_REL` input on a RELA target).** No compiler on the
  matrix makes one. *Recommendation:* refuse by name (A-R4). Converting
  needs the addend read from the bytes, which is easy, but no line needs
  it.
- **Q9 (GCC LTO sections at `-r`).** *Recommendation:* refuse (RV-R9)
  until a GCC LTO build needs `-r`. Then measure what GCC's driver does
  at `-r -flto`: it runs the plugin.
- **Q10 (MT-16 anchors through `-r`).** A merge by name breaks the
  section-ordinal anchor. *Recommendation:* treat MT-16 anchors as
  unanchored in a multi-unit container (MT-16 as amended). Any future
  kind that must survive `-r` records the offset within the section
  (kind 0x10 for `incr.md`).
- **Q11 (ODR at `-r`).** *Recommendation:* yes, the same checks as a
  final link over the component's own units (MT-30a). The component's
  build is where its own violation is first known. The final link
  checks again.
- **Q12 (`--build-id` at `-r`).** *Recommendation:* accept it and write
  nothing (mold). The note of an intermediate is dropped by every final
  link (`spec/input.md`), and clang passes `--build-id` on every `-r`
  line.
- **Q13 (`.llvm_addrsig`).** *Recommendation:* drop it at `-r` (L-R1).
  An empty one, which is what all three references write, claims that no
  symbol is address-significant.
- **Q14 (detecting mold's mislinks).** A mold `-r` output has
  `SHN_ABS` symbols whose value equals a power of two and whose size is
  that of a variable. *Recommendation:* no linker rule, because it
  cannot be told apart from a real absolute symbol. The lane keeps
  `skip(mold)` with the reason (D.2).
- **Q15 (the pre-link idiom).** *Recommendation:*
  `--force-group-allocation` in RL-8 (A.4), and a sentence in the
  user-facing docs: localize after `-r` only with groups dissolved.
  WP-176's `comdat-mismatch` turns the silent abort into a refusal by
  name.

## Appendix B. Not verified

- **The references' handling of a debug record into a dropped COMDAT
  copy at `-r`** (I-R7, Q5): the corpus had none.
- **`.note.GNU-stack` merging when inputs mix** (L-R6a).
- **A type conflict among pieces of one name at `-r`** (`PROGBITS` and
  `NOBITS` and the like): assumed to follow L-49, not measured.
- **`SHF_LINK_ORDER` keyed by the linked-to section** (L-R1): the flag
  is kept by all three, but a case linking to two different output
  sections was not built.
- **`-gsplit-dwarf` through `-r`**: it runs, but the skeleton units and
  `DW_AT_dwo_name` in the `-r` output were not read (the grep found
  nothing, probably a form mismatch), and gdb was not run on it.
- **lldb against GNU ld's direct link**: it differs only in a libc alias
  name, on mortar's routes, and was not traced further.
- **A fat GCC LTO object at `-r`** (RV-R9: the measured one was slim).
- **`.symver` at `-r` beyond names**: whether a `foo` reference and a
  `foo@@V` definition become one symbol inside GNU ld's `-r`.
- **`-d` with commons of several alignments** (only the size and the
  section were read).
- **`--defsym`** at `-r`: only that it adds a symbol, not its section or
  value form.
- **i386 REL write-back overflow** (a 16-bit field that the piece offset
  overflows): no reference case was built; A-R2's
  `relocation-out-of-range` is the rule.
- **x86-64 and i386 debug transcripts** (gdb/lldb ran on AArch64 only).
- **The `-O0` corpus through the whole D.3 matrix**: only through
  debug/ (AArch64) and none-overlap/ (three targets).
- **mortar's own final link of a 400-object `-r` output** (E measures
  only the references' `-r`).
- **BusyBox with mortar's `-r`**: it cannot run until RL-7.
