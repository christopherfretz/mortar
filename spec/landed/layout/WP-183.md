# WP-183 (RL-3): layout at `-r`

The cut is record `spec/landed/layout/WP-154.md` §F; the types are
record `spec/landed/core/WP-181.md` §A. The sizes follow the
supervisor's `rl-sizes` rulings S1 to S16 (S12 to S16 answer this
package's questions C1 to C4), which RL-4 (WP-184) received in the
same words. `-r` stays refused until RL-7 joins the parts: no final
link runs any of this code.

## A. What landed

- `mortar_layout::relocatable::layout` (`crates/mortar_layout/src/relocatable.rs`)
  computes a `RelocatableLayout { assignment, layout, eh_removed }` in
  eight steps:
  1. the role of each input section;
  2. the kept groups;
  3. `out_of` by L-R1's key, with L-R1a's type, flags and alignment;
  4. the pieces (L-R4, L-R5);
  5. the removed FDEs (L-R6, as data) and the output relocation
     records;
  6. the chunks and L-R8's order;
  7. the `.symtab` entries (I-R4, S4, S14);
  8. the file offsets.
- **Signature change (S16).** `layout` takes `split: &Split` and
  `notes: NoteSizes { property, build_id }` beyond WP-181's arguments.
  RL-7 computes the two note sizes with `mortar_synth` (the property
  plan and the build-id plan): `mortar_layout` does not depend on
  `mortar_synth`.
- **Pure sizes** (`pub`, for RL-7 to test against RL-4's):
  - `strtab_size` over `EntryName`, with `STRTAB_FIXED` (S2);
  - `shstrtab_size` over `SectionName` (S3);
  - `symtab_entries` and `symtab_size` (S4);
  - `symtab_shndx_size` and `needs_xindex` (S5);
  - `relocs_size` and `reloc_entry_size` (S6);
  - `group_size` (S8);
  - `eh_frame_size` (S12);
  - `file_header_size`, `section_header_size` and `word_align`;
  - `eh_removed`, L-R6's decision as a function of `Split` and of
    which sections are dropped.
- `mortar_core::check::relocatable` fills `validate` with L-R10's
  readings. Two of L-R10's added rules need facts the tables do not
  hold, so they are separate functions taking those facts:
  - `validate_groups(layout, cx, kept)`: the kept groups and their
    placed members, read from the inputs;
  - `validate_relocs(layout, cx, records)`: the output records per
    output section (I-R5).
  `placed_at_r(kind)` is `-r`'s rule 1 set of kinds: `Regular`,
  `Nobits`, `Note`, `Debug` and `Merge`.
- `assign.rs`: `combine` and `type_name` became `pub(crate)`. That is
  the only change on a final link's path, and it changes no behaviour.
  `order.rs` is unchanged: `-r` has its own order (L-R8) inside
  `relocatable.rs`.

## B. Readings and choices

1. **Merge sections are placed whole.** `SectionKind::is_placed` is
   false for `Merge` because a final link places their fragments. At
   `-r` (L-R5) they are pieces like any other, so rule 1 at `-r` uses
   `placed_at_r`, not `is_placed`.
2. **A kept group with no placed member** (all its members dropped by
   the line) keeps its `SHT_GROUP` chunk, of size 4 (the flag word
   alone), at its own input position. L-R3 and L-R8 do not cover this
   case; the alternative is to drop the group.
3. **The header chunks** (`FileHeader`, `SectionHeaders`) have
   `sh_type` `PROGBITS`, flags 0 and alignment the word size. They have
   no section index.
4. **NOBITS chunks** get an aligned offset and take no file bytes, as
   L-R8 says.
5. **Flags L-R1a cannot carry** are refused by name, as `section flags
   0x… of NAME at -r` (`refused`, class `SectionFlag`). For example,
   AArch64's `SHF_AARCH64_PURECODE` and GNU's `SHF_GNU_MBIND`. Known
   flags: `WAX`, `MERGE`, `STRINGS`, `INFO_LINK`, `LINK_ORDER`, `GROUP`,
   `TLS`, `COMPRESSED`, `GNU_RETAIN` and `EXCLUDE`, plus
   `X86_64_LARGE` on x86-64.
6. **A `SHF_LINK_ORDER` mix cannot meet** in one output section: the
   linked-to output section is part of L-R1's key. L-R1a's
   `section-conflict` for that mix therefore never fires. The
   `SHF_EXCLUDE` mix does fire.
7. **A chain of `SHF_LINK_ORDER` sections** is resolved in rounds. A
   cycle, or a linked-to section that is not in the output, is refused
   by name.
8. **The two notes.**
   - The merged property note is a synthetic chunk at its first input's
     position, `NOTE`, `ALLOC`, word-aligned, and its inputs are
     `Consumed` (S10).
   - A planned property note with no input property note is an
     internal error.
   - An input property note with relocations is refused by name.
   - The build-id note comes right after the file header (S9).
9. **Locals (I-R4).** A local defined in `.note.GNU-stack` is not
   written (the merged note is made by the `-r` itself). A local inside
   a removed FDE is not written. The type-unit signature defined in a
   kept group section is written (L-R3). Every other local of a group
   section is not written.
10. **Globals (S14).** A defined global whose section has no output at
    `-r` is an internal error, and so is a `Shared` definition.
11. **S13.** A `Stack` section, or an `Excluded` section with
    `SHF_EXCLUDE` other than `.llvm_addrsig`, the call-graph profile,
    the input build-id note and GCC's LTO sections, is an internal error
    naming L-R1.
12. **Refused by name, for a later package:**
    - `-d` with `-r` (RL-11);
    - `--force-group-allocation` (RL-8);
    - the Mason container (RL-12);
    - `.eh_frame` in a group, or with `SHF_GNU_RETAIN` or
      `SHF_LINK_ORDER` (S1);
    - a 64-bit `.eh_frame` record length;
    - a relocation section of the other type (`SHT_REL` on ELF64,
      `SHT_RELA` on i386).

## C. Spec edits

  names the KEPT INPUT `SHT_GROUP` section the chunk is written from".
- `spec/layout.md` §24:
  - L-R4: `.eh_frame` is not placed by L-R4's alignment; its bytes are
    contiguous (S1).
  - L-R6: a paragraph "In bytes" that states S1 and S12. It covers the
    synthetic chunk, the content with removed FDEs cut out, no padding,
    the size, the one output section, and the relocation records.
  - L-R1a: a compressed piece is placed at its decompressed size and
    alignment (S15).

## D. Tests

`crates/mortar_layout/tests/relocatable.rs` builds objects with a writer
of its own. The `Inputs`, `Resolution` (with `-r`'s classification as
S13 gives it), `Liveness` and `Split` are hand-made. Every test checks
the result with `Layout::check`, `validate_groups` and
`validate_relocs`. The tests:

- `r_section_order`: the probe's two C objects (x86-64) and their order,
  offsets and sizes. `r_section_order_i386` does the same with `SHT_REL`.
- `r_section_order_against_lld` (tool-backed: gcc and ld.lld on the
  host): the same order as lld's `-r`, except for L-R8's two documented
  differences (`.note.GNU-stack`, and `.strtab` before `.shstrtab`).
- `r_names_kept`, `r_retain_split`, `r_section_conflicts`,
  `r_exclude_kept_and_addrsig_dropped`, `r_stack_note`,
  `r_compressed_input`, `r_link_order`: L-R1 and L-R1a.
- `r_group_comdat`, `r_group_member_outside_group_kept_apart`,
  `r_group_refusals`: L-R2 and L-R3.
- `r_many_sections`: `.symtab_shndx`.
- `r_eh_frame_dropped_fde`, `r_eh_frame_cies_kept`: L-R6, S1 and S12.
- `r_property_and_build_id_notes`: S9 and S10.
- `r_globals`: S2 and S14.
- `r_refusals`, `r_pure_sizes`.
- `r_deterministic`: every pool schedule gives the same result.
- `r_validate_*`: each reading of L-R10 against a layout that violates
  it.
