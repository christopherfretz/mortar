# WP-032g — performance: parallel regions, the small-link regime

Branch `wp/032g-perf-par`, cut from main 543fd4e (not merged with main
since; it merges cleanly with main 426d140). Crates: `mortar_core::par`
and the call sites of its helpers in every crate (one-line annotations;
those in `mortar_image` and `mortar_scan` are a commit of their own).

## Method

- Workloads: static `g++ -static` link lines (`hello-c-static`,
  `hello-cxx-static`; protoc `cxx-medium-g`, `cxx-medium`, `synthetic-*`,
  each made static from the hello template), and one x86-64 link (q.cc
  of `tests/x64.rs` `CXX_THREADS`). Interleaved runs (A B A B ...), the
  host shared, load 10–16 on 12 CPUs (stated per table).
- Byte identity at every commit: output, `-Map`, stderr and status of 36
  links hashed together (5 workloads × threads {1, 8, 8 +
  `MORTAR_SCHED_SEED=977`} × store {staged, resident}, plus the two
  hello workloads × {1, 8, 8 + seed}). The pair compared: each commit's
  binary against 543fd4e's. Identical at 45d3cfd, db06cf6 and 0710c4b.
- `MORTAR_STEPS=1` now prints one line per parallel region:
  `mortar: steps: par <file>:<line>: <ms> ms, <n> item, <w> worker, <s> spawned`
  (the call site from `#[track_caller]` on every helper).

## Profile before (543fd4e)

A link is a sequence of ~90–120 parallel regions (protoc 113, hello-cxx
92). Each spawned and joined `threads − 1` scoped threads, ~280 µs wall
and ~360 µs CPU per region at 8 threads. Spawns per link at 2/4/8/12
threads were protoc 102/279/583/859, hello-cxx 73/205/448/678 and
synthetic-objects 71/208/461/713. At 8 threads on hello-cxx the regions
added up to 42 ms of a 61 ms link, and most of that was spawn and join.
No safe persistent pool exists (a scoped borrow cannot outlive its
region), so the fix spawns fewer threads.

## Fixes

| commit | fix |
|---|---|
| 45d3cfd | `par`: region lines; workers spawned lazily in a tree; `Pool::unit_cost` |
| db06cf6 | call sites state their unit cost (all crates but image/scan) |
| 0710c4b | the same in `mortar_image` and `mortar_scan` (four lines) |

**Lazy tree spawn** (`Team` in `par.rs`). The caller spawns one worker
and then works. Each spawned worker first spawns up to two more
(`FANOUT`), while fewer than `workers` have started, and then takes
items. Without a seed, a worker spawns only while items are left
untaken, so a region that is over before a thread starts spawns no more.
With a seed, every worker is spawned (the schedules the seed explores
stay the eager ones).

This is not a timing decision that can change a result. Every schedule
it produces is one the eager spawn already allowed, namely the one where
the late threads started after all items were taken. The results of the
helpers do not depend on the schedule (`spec/model.md` §6, tested by
`check_schedules`). The first panic payload is resumed on the caller
(`catch_unwind` per worker).

**`Pool::unit_cost(ns)`** sizes a region deterministically. A call site
states the cost of one unit in ns, and the region runs on
`min(threads, units × ns / 200 µs)` workers, at least 1. `WORKER_NS =
200 000`, about the spawn and join cost at 8 threads. The constant per
site is the 1-thread median per unit on the seven workloads, raised
where the median left a large region under-parallelized. A region with
too little work runs on the caller. The cost is ignored under
`MORTAR_SCHED_SEED`. Sites whose units are shard counts or
data-dependent stay unannotated (eight in `mortar_merge/merge.rs`,
`store.rs` 1104/1120/1165, `walk.rs` 489, `fixed.rs` 416, `sites.rs`
128). `spec/model.md` §6 gains one paragraph: the worker count is a
schedule.

## Measured and not landed

- **Early exit** (item 6): the binary leaves the front and the store to
  the exit (`mem::forget`; nothing is lost, because the output is
  committed in `link_image` and `Deferred` is disarmed on success).
  Within noise at 1 and 8 threads on hello-c, hello-cxx, protoc and
  objects (e.g. protoc 8T 289.0 → 242.9 median but min 172.3 → 182.5;
  1T 529.2 → 521.5). Dropped. The 9–17 ms gap between `--time-phases`
  total and process wall on protoc is the exec and the kernel's
  teardown of the address space, not the drops.
- **Intern entries 56 → 16 bytes** (item 4): on protoc the table holds
  46 565 keys (~4.3 MB) and both numberings take 11 ms of a 540 ms
  1-thread link. A 16-byte cell cannot hold the key, so every probe
  that matches a hash would re-read the key from its first mention's
  symbol table. Below the bar; not attempted.
- **Archive members resident as symbol tables only** (item 2): an
  estimate from hello-cxx's map. 14.8 MB of archive members, 5.3 MB in
  the 585 extracted ones. Of the 9.5 MB not extracted, the ELF header,
  section headers and symbol and string tables are 3.8 MB. At most
  ~5 MB of the 15.3 MB resident would not be read (wave C is 4 ms of a
  41 ms 1-thread link). Doing it needs a second read after resolve, and
  the members' `Object`s are made at the walk from a `View` over the
  parts as they then are: they would have to be parsed again after the
  second read and replaced in the model. That is a change across
  `mortar_elf::View`, the store, the walk and the driver, with
  `spec/input.md` §11 (two reads, `input-changed` across them). Not
  started.

## Findings

- **Wave A does not scale** (item 7). On synthetic-objects (1006
  objects) the head-and-tail reads of `store.rs:1104` take 50–70 ms at
  every thread count from 1 to 12 under load 13 (31–66 ms at 1 thread).
  A context-switch profile at 8 threads puts ~25% of the switches in
  the fault path under `Reader::read_into_own` and ~12% under
  `__libc_calloc`. The leading hypothesis is contention on the address
  space lock between new buffers (one `vec![0; n]` per read) and the
  page faults filling them. The likely fix is one allocation per batch,
  split into the reads' buffers. `glibc.malloc.arena_max=1` changes
  nothing.
- **`LinkSites::new`** walks every relocation of the live code again, at
  the scan's own per-record cost: protoc 18 ms, synthetic-objects 69 ms
  of a ~500 ms 1-thread link (`scan walk` is 68 ms on the same
  records). Only branch records (40% on objects) matter. The scan
  already classifies every record and could keep the branch records'
  places. That is the scan's inner loop (WP-032f), so it is left to
  that package.
- **Small links** (hello-cxx 1T, 41 ms): input 18 ms (17.3 MB read, of
  which 15.3 MB stays resident; `walk.read` 8.9, interning 2.5, the
  search 2.3), image 7.9, layout 4.9, resolve 3.6, scan 2.6. At the
  default thread count on a loaded host, more threads cost more wall
  than they save: hello-cxx default 67 ms against 47 ms at 1 thread
  (load 12). A link-wide cap from the input size would be the next
  step after `unit_cost`.
