# WP-005 — the archive reader

`spec/archive.md` and `mortar_elf::archive` (`crates/mortar_elf/src/archive.rs`):
a zero-copy reader over the bytes of a regular or thin archive, with tests
in `crates/mortar_elf/tests/archive/`.

## What it does

- `Archive::parse` reads the magic and the special members at the front:
  the symbol index (GNU `/`, 64-bit `/SYM64/`, BSD `__.SYMDEF*` in 32 and
  64 bits, all decoded) and the long-name table. Nothing else is read
  until asked for.
- `Archive::members()` walks the ordinary members; `Archive::member_at`
  reads one from a header offset of the index. Names: GNU short names,
  long-name references, BSD `#1/` names, unterminated BSD short names.
- Thin archives give paths; a member of a regular archive that GNU `ar`
  nested in a thin one (`/<n>:<origin>`) is given as the archive's path
  and the member's header offset in it.
- One `Copy` error enum; every variant but `NotAnArchive` carries the
  header offset. No allocation anywhere in the module.

## Decisions the spec records

- **Format-independent grammar.** There is no "GNU mode" and "BSD mode":
  every name form is read wherever it appears. `llvm-ar --format=bsd`
  writes `/SYM64/` with `#1/` names, so the forms do mix.
- **Strict where no tool writes it.** Trailing bytes after the last
  member, sizes that are not plain decimal, a long name without `/\n`, a
  special member out of place, a name with a space and no terminating
  `/`, a NUL in a name: refused by name. GNU `ar` reads most of these;
  `llvm-ar` refuses most. Where the two would list different members or
  names, mortar refuses (`spec/archive.md` §10).
- **One leniency:** the missing padding byte of the last member, which
  GNU `ar` reads and `llvm-ar` refuses. All of the member is there.
- **A name field that starts with `/` or `#1/` ends at its first space.**
  Found by the L1 test, not by reading documentation: GNU `ar` 2.44 writes
  `/79            /` for a thin member whose name is 15 bytes long.
- **`parse` examines at most two headers** (the first, and the one after a
  symbol index) and reports their errors; the rest are reported lazily.
- **The BSD index is read as little-endian** (the byte order of mortar's
  targets). GNU `ar`/`nm` do not see the index `llvm-ar` writes, so
  LLVM's tools are the oracle for it.

## Evidence

Gate: `source scripts/env.sh && MORTAR_REQUIRE_TOOLS=1 cargo xtask ci --quick`,
clean. Tools: GNU binutils 2.44, LLVM 22.1.8 (`mortar_testutil::tool`
prefers 22; the cases of §10 were also run by hand with 19.1.7, with the
same results), gcc 14.

- **L0**: 50 tests of the spec's sections on hand-built bytes
  (`archive/spec.rs`), 2 unit tests in the module.
- **L1, the system's archives** (`s9_system_archives_*`): the 70 archives
  under `/usr/lib` (glibc 2.41: `libc.a`, `libm-2.41.a`, `libmvec.a`, ...;
  GCC 14 for aarch64 and the i686 and x86-64 cross compilers: `libgcc.a`,
  `libgcc_eh.a`, `libstdc++.a`, `libsupc++.a`, `libgfortran.a`, the
  sanitizer runtimes, ...); `libm.a` (a linker script) and `libmcheck.a`
  (an object) are skipped as not archives. 8 332 members: names, order and
  count against `ar t`, sizes against `ar tv`, content offsets against
  `ar tO`, 77 351 072 bytes of content against `ar p`. 83 071 index
  entries against `nm --print-armap`, each symbol with the name of the
  member `member_at` finds at its offset. All equal.
- **L1, built at test time**: by GNU `ar` 9 archives (`rcs`, `rcS`, empty,
  no objects, a name four times, thin, thin in another directory with
  relative and absolute paths, regular and thin nested in thin, thin in
  regular), 7 of them also compared with LLVM's tools; by `llvm-ar` 9
  archives (`--format=gnu` with and without index, thin, `/SYM64/`, nested,
  `--format=bsd` with and without index, `--format=darwin`, `--format=bsd`
  with `/SYM64/`), 5 of them also compared with GNU's tools. 30 comparisons,
  161 members, 147 index entries. All equal.
- **L1, the fixture builder**: the hand-built seeds and a hand-built thin
  archive are read alike by the tools (11 comparisons).
- **L1, `ar x`**: every hundredth member of `libc.a` extracted and
  compared byte for byte.
- **§10**: 13 inputs on which the tools differ from each other or from
  mortar, pinned with the outcome of each of the three; and the two
  differences in what they write (§7.3, the stray `/` of §4).
- **Hostile input**: every prefix of 7 seed fixtures (4 462 inputs, with
  `member_at` at every offset of each); 210 000 mutants (30 000 per seed,
  one to three mutations each from a xorshift generator: random and
  telling bytes, bit flips, extreme words in both widths and byte orders,
  decimal fields, truncation, moved runs), of which 147 406 still parse.
  No panic; the walk never exceeds the bound the input's size gives.
- **Speed** (release build, this machine, not a gate): `parse` of
  `libc.a` 35 ns; walking the 7 414 index entries of `libstdc++.a` 205 µs;
  walking the 2 022 members of `libc.a` 88 µs.

## Open questions

1. **Policy for `Content::ExternalMember`** (`spec/input.md`,
   `spec/resolve.md`). The reader gives the path and the offset; GNU ld
   links such archives, lld refuses them, mold silently takes nothing.
   Suggested: do as GNU ld does (one more file read and a `member_at`).
2. **The index is trusted, not checked against the members.** A stale
   index (members replaced without `ranlib`) is not detectable here. Whether
   the linker resolves from the index (GNU ld, fast) or from the members'
   symbol tables (lld, mold) is `spec/resolve.md`'s decision; the reader
   serves both.
3. **Strictness that could refuse a file some writer produces.** Trailing
   bytes after the last member and `AmbiguousName` are the two refusals
   GNU `ar` does not share. No writer known to me produces either; if one
   turns up, relaxing is a spec change with a test.
4. **BSD index byte order** is fixed little-endian. A big-endian target
   would need the caller to say so.
5. **Header fields not exposed**: modification time, owner, group, mode.
   `mortar_incr` may want the time; archives written deterministically
   (`ar D`, the default on Debian) have zero there, so content hashes are
   the better change detector anyway.
6. **`member_at` cannot tell a header from member bytes that look like
   one** (§7.1); neither can the tools. A linker that takes offsets only
   from the index of the same archive is exposed only to a hostile index.
7. **The 64-bit index test depends on `SYM64_THRESHOLD`**, an environment
   variable LLVM's archive writer reads for its own tests. An `llvm-ar`
   that ignores it fails `s9_archives_built_by_llvm_ar` by assertion
   rather than silently testing the 32-bit form; the hand-built `/SYM64/`
   fixture, which GNU `nm` and `llvm-nm` both read, does not depend on it.
8. **The §10 pins are tied to tool versions.** A binutils or LLVM that
   changes one of the recorded behaviors fails `s10_*`; the fix is to
   update the table.
