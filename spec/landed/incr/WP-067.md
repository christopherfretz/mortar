# WP-067: L10, the random edit-sequence lane (H-3)

Record of WP-067: `cargo xtask l10`, the lane of `spec/incr.md` IN-63
(`docs/VALIDATION.md` L10; `spec/harness.md` "L10"). The lane exists
before the update does: today every `--incremental` link is a full link
(reasons `no-state`, `small-output`, `options-changed`,
`output-changed`), so every sequence is FLAGGED ("only fall-backs"), and
what the lane judges now is incremental mode's full links (exit,
verifier, equivalence against the plain link, the transcript) and the
dry run's predictions (the IN-63a oracle over each step's `delta` lines).
When the update lands, the same steps judge it with no change to the
lane.

## 2. `judge_step` (for H-1)

```python
# tests/l10/judge.py
Tools(xtask, runner, run_timeout=60, args=())   # runner: [] or a qemu prefix; args: the program's
Link(d, out, map_, status, diags, report=None)  # a link's directory, output and map names, exit status,
                                                #   normalized diagnostics, report path
judge_step(tools, x, f, base, new) -> Verdict   # x: the subject's link; f: the plain full link;
                                                #   base/new: the previous and this step's
                                                #   --incremental=full links (the oracle's pair), or None
Verdict.checks   [(name, 'pass' | 'FAIL ...' | 'n/a ...')], names in CHECKS =
                 ('exit', 'verify', 'equiv', 'run', 'oracle')
Verdict.reason   the report's reason ('update' or the fall-back's name)
Verdict.ok / first_failure() / line() / times (seconds per check)
```

Nothing in it is L10's: H-1 can add IN-79's checks (the state
invariant, the dirty ranges) as further entries of `checks`.

## 7. Findings

### 7.1 Against today's linker

Every finding below is SUSPECTED: none was compared against the bytes
a spec rule prescribes. None is a wrong program and none is a verifier
rejection: in every case X (the `--incremental` link, here always a
full link) and F (the plain link) each pass `cargo xtask plumb` alone,
and the two programs print and exit alike; it is `plumb --equiv` X
against F (IN-1 (C)) that finds differences. Whether the linker or the
equivalence rule is wrong is the question each one asks.

**L-1. aarch64 clang objects: `.eh_frame` of an incremental-mode full link
differs from the plain link's (`P-E3`, `$d`).** gen200 and corpus40,
`clang-a64-O0-g-fs-static` (every seed, at the base link) and
`clang-a64-O2-g-nofs-static` (outside the matrix, seed 1, base);
`clang-a64-O2-nog-nofs-static` passes its base. 195 findings (corpus40),
920-933 (gen200), all `verifier rule P-E3: the local '$d' has a value no
local of the full link corresponds to`. corpus40 seed 1: X's `.eh_frame`
is 0x276e4 bytes, F's 0x24d44 (10,656 bytes more), and X has 454 `$d`
locals in `.eh_frame` (section 8) to F's 414; every other section's `$d`
count is equal. Reading: the incremental-mode full link keeps
`.eh_frame` records the plain link drops or merges (CIEs, or FDEs), and
the mapping symbols clang puts on them survive with values F has no
counterpart for. Either the IN-1 equivalence of `.eh_frame` does not
hold for incremental mode on aarch64 (mortar), or P-E3 should not ask
mapping symbols in `.eh_frame` to correspond (plumb). Not reached by gcc
(no `$d` in `.eh_frame`), x86-64 or i386 (no mapping symbols). Replay:
`cargo xtask l10 --replay tests/l10/regressions/gen200.clang-a64-O0-g-fs-static.seed1.json`
(minimized: the base link alone).

**L-2. `.pad.relro` exists in one of X and F only (`P-E2`, `P-E1`).**
corpus40 `clang-x64-O2-nog-nofs-static` seed 2 at step 6 (`e6`, a member
added to `Widget`, after `e9`'s third hop): X has `chunk synthetic
.pad.relro` (0x428 bytes at 0x5a7bd8), F has none; findings
`P-E2: the synthetic chunk '.pad.relro' (0) is only in the update` and
`P-E1: program header 10 covers other sections`, `program header 2 covers
other sections` (the RELRO segment and its load segment). gen200
`clang-a64-O2-nog-nofs-static` seed 1 at step 25 (`e10`): the converse,
`'.pad.relro' (0) is only in the full link`, and program headers 8 and
2. The pad is decided from addresses, and the incremental-mode layout's
addresses differ from the plain link's (IN-72's slack); a chunk that
exists in one layout and not the other then fails the chunk-by-chunk
comparison. Either the incremental-mode full link should decide the pad
as the plain link does (or always emit it, possibly empty), or IN-1 (C)
should allow the RELRO pad to differ like other slack (plumb). Replays:
`cargo xtask l10 --replay tests/l10/regressions/corpus40.clang-x64-O2-nog-nofs-static.seed2.json`
(minimized: 2 steps, `e9` then `e6`, after the base);

### 7.2 Against `tests/incr-oracle/oracle.py` (not this package's file)

Two defects, both met first by L10 and worked around in `tests/l10/
judge.py` with adapters (the oracle is used as a library, not copied);
proposed patches for the oracle's owner:

1. **Thin archives.** `Link.file_bytes` reads an archive member at its
   offset in the archive; a thin archive holds only the member headers,
   so `elf()` asserts. Proposed: in `Link.file_bytes`, when the archive's
   magic is `!<thin>\n`, read the member header at the offset, resolve
   its name (`//` table for `/N`), and read the file the name gives
   relative to the archive's directory (judge.py `_thin_member`,
   `_file_bytes_thin`).
2. **Member identity by offset.** `Link` keys a member by `(path,
   member, offset)`. When an earlier member of the same archive changes
   size, every later member moves, and the oracle reads them as changed
   files and asks the report to predict their units ("MISS unit of a
   changed file"); mortar (IN-24) and its report identify a member by
   archive and name. Proposed: key members by `(path, member, ordinal
   among the members of that name)` and keep the offset only for
   reading (judge.py `_link_init_by_name`). Three of the lane's first
   failures were this (corpus40 e1 and extraction, gen200 common-ref);
   none was mortar's.

### 7.3 Spec findings

1. **IN-12: no report for an ignored `--incremental`.** Under IN-12
   (PIE today) mortar warns and links plainly and writes no
   `--incremental-report` file, so a harness has no `incremental: full
   (<reason>)` line to read; L10 takes the reason from the warning
   (`incremental-unsupported`). Proposed, after "links plainly, writing
   no state;": "with `--incremental-report`, the report's one line is
   `incremental: full (incremental-unsupported)` with `changed=0
   refilled=0`, so every `--incremental` link states its reason."
2. **IN-10b vs IN-63's large project.** IN-63 names protoc for L10, but
   protoc's reports exceed the bound (31,733 `symbol` lines, 26,208
   `unit` lines), so IN-63a's oracle cannot judge a protoc step and the
   lane records it `n/a`. Proposed, IN-10b's last sentence but one: "The
   bound keeps every oracle-lane report whole (its largest kind is 2,137
   units); the large projects of IN-63 (protoc, the go/no-go workload)
   exceed it, and their steps are judged without the oracle until a
   harness option lifts the bound."
3. **IN-1 (C) and synthetic pads/mapping symbols** (L-1, L-2): the rule
   should say whether the RELRO pad and the mapping symbols of a
   section mortar rewrites (`.eh_frame`) belong to the compared layout.
   No text proposed: it depends on which side L-1 and L-2 are fixed.
4. **IN-63's floor sentence.** "the floor ... on same-size edits passes,
   with its steps counted as updates": WP-061's floor takes one changed
   loose object or thin member; a same-size change of a regular archive's
   member, or a step with no change, is refused by name
   (`layout-changed`, `0 changed`). Proposed: "... passes, every step an
   update or a refusal the floor names."
