### What `mortar_synth` consumes

- `Phase::eh`, an `EhFrames`:
  - `sections[s]` is an `EhSection { section, size, records: Span, terminator }`;
  - `records[r]` is a `Record { offset, len, relocs: Span, kind }`;
  - `Kind::Fde(Fde { cie, code, range, lsda })`, where `cie` is the record index of its CIE in its section and `code` is `Code::{Section { section, offset }, Dropped, Absolute, Undefined}`;
  - `Kind::Cie(Cie { class, version, fde_enc, lsda_enc, personality, signal, z })`.
- `Phase::eh_relocs[s]` holds each section's relocations, sorted by offset. `Record::relocs` indexes into it, and each relocation already has its resolved `Target`.
- `EhFrames::keepers(live)` gives the kept CIE per class (S-44). `refs::eh_ref` gives the value of a reference into `.eh_frame` (S-50).
- `Phase::split` and `Phase::meta` go to layout (L-85 `Merged` chunks) and `order::offsets` to the image. The image writes a sub-range of a merged chunk through `Order::frags_in`.

### Bytes and the staged store (WP-021b)

- Phase 60 reads three kinds of section bytes:
  - every kept `Merge` section that has an output section;
  - every kept `.eh_frame` (`EhFrame`);
  - the relocation section that applies to each `.eh_frame`.
- It reads no other bytes.
- `phase::read` gets them:
  - `Inputs::resident(file, range)` when the store has them resident (borrowed);
  - otherwise `Inputs::copy_to` into a `Vec` of its own;
  - for a file without a store entry (tests), the object's bytes.
- The owned buffers are held for the whole of phase 60: interning compares bytes across all sections. `cut::cut` and `eh::parse` take `&[u8]`, so either source works.
- `Phase` keeps no input bytes. A fragment's representative is named by `Frag { section, offset, size }`, and the image reads it again when it writes.

### Deviations and decisions

- **S-28 step 4.** It "buckets by section". `merge` sets one bit per representative occurrence (an `AtomicU64` bitset over occurrences) and numbers by popcount prefix in occurrence order. Occurrences are in key order, so this yields the same `FragId`s; it is one pass over `total / 64` words.
- **S-12.** "More than 2^32 − 1 pieces in a section" cannot happen. A section over `u32::MAX` bytes is refused first (`SectionTooLarge`; piece offsets are `u32`), and such a section has at most 2^32 − 1 pieces. `TooManyPiecesInLink` refuses more than 2^32 − 1 occurrences in the link (`FragId` and `piece_frag` indices).
- **S-6** adds `SHF_COMPRESSED`: such a section is placed whole, since its bytes are not pieces. A compressed `.eh_frame` is refused (`feature`, `SHF_COMPRESSED .eh_frame`).
- **S-43/S-45.** CIE classes are numbered by one sequential first-occurrence walk over all CIEs, keyed by bytes plus (offset, type, addend, target) per relocation. The rule says "after resolution, over live CIEs" by the S-28 method. Targets here are already resolved, and `keepers(live)` picks the least live member of a class, so the kept CIEs and FDE pointers are the same. There are few CIEs (19 classes for 3810 CIEs in the AArch64 libraries).
- **Terminators** are not `Split` pieces: `EhSection::terminator` gives the offset. S-48 drops them.
- **A strong undefined FDE target** gives `Code::Undefined`, which is dead like the weak case. Resolve has already refused the undefined symbol, unless it is allowed.
- **Narrowings of S-37 and S-38** (`What`, all `feature`):
  - `uleb128` and `sleb128` pointers are refused;
  - application bits above `pcrel` are refused;
  - `indirect` is refused on the `R` encoding;
  - the relocation at a pointer must be `Pc` for `pcrel`, otherwise `Abs` (`AbsWord` or `AbsNarrow`), with the pointer's width, at its first byte (`PointerReloc`: "`R_NAME` at an .eh_frame pointer of another encoding");
  - two relocations at one pointer give `TwoRelocs`;
  - a relocation of a record's length or CIE-pointer word gives `RelocInHeader`;
  - an FDE whose initial location resolves to a common, shared or linker-defined symbol gives `FdeCodeElsewhere`;
  - `P` and `L` accept `0xff` (omit).

### spec/synth.md edits (each provable by the corpus)

1. S-37: GCC's non-PIC x86-64 code (`-fno-pic -fno-PIE`) encodes FDE, LSDA and personality pointers as `udata4` (`0x03`) with `R_X86_64_32`.
2. S-38: the class is `Pc` for `pcrel` and `AbsWord` or `AbsNarrow` otherwise, with the pointer's width, at its first byte. Personality and LSDA pointers follow the same rule. The previous text ("`Pc` or `AbsWord`") would refuse the case in edit 1.

## Performance (`examples/merge_bench.rs`, release, shared host at load 23 to 31)

`merge` covers the cut, hash, partition, intern, number and resolve steps; `offsets` is S-29. Times are medians of 5 runs.

| input | 1 thread | 2 | 4 | 8 | S-30 (sum of steps: 1 / 4 / 8) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| synthetic: 10^7 pieces, 535 MB, 2,408,408 fragments | 2182 ms | 1202 | 610 | 389 (2nd run: 331) | 2245 / 720–857 / 709 |
| `cxx-medium-g` `.debug_str`: 990,853 pieces, 64.2 MB, 95,321 fragments, 10.21 MB out | 109 ms | 55 | 38 | 29 (1st run: 22) | about 60 at 4 |
| `offsets`, synthetic | 21 ms | 27 | 26 | 25 | S-29: 2–3 |

The `cxx-medium-g` piece, fragment and output-size counts equal S-30's exactly.

**Memory**, synthetic, from the process's first merge at 8 threads, measured as VmHWM beyond the RSS after the input is built:

- Peak: +339 MB (33.9 bytes per piece). S-30's formula gives 280 + 108 = 388 MB.
- Kept after the phase: +147 MB. The formula gives 80 MB for the `Split` rows, plus `frags` (16 bytes per fragment, 38 MB) and allocator slack.

Where the formula is off:

1. It leaves out the cut's own offset list (4 bytes per occurrence). That list lives beside the hashes until the rows are built, and then becomes `piece_off`.
2. The per-step lifetimes do not overlap the way the formula assumes. Hashes and the partition records (8 + 8) are freed before `piece_frag` (4) is written. So the measured peak per occurrence is about 23 bytes plus fragments, not 28 plus fragments.
3. Per fragment, the cost is about 44 bytes. That is close to the formula, but the table's load factor is not fixed: tables grow by doubling, so it lies between 0.25 and 0.5.

Later merges in the same process read lower peaks (142 to 314 MB) because the heap is reused; the bench reports them only as "heap reused". Against mold: the protoc `.debug_str` merge takes 38 ms at 4 threads, against 0.72 s for mold's whole link (S-30).

## Follow-ups

1. `crates/mortar_resolve/src/sections.rs:143`: the S-6 cases (`SHF_WRITE`/`EXECINSTR`, relocated, TLS, entsize > 2^16, compressed) should make a section `Regular`, not `Merge`. The alternative is for the layout and core to consult `Phase::whole`. Until then, `phase::run` places them whole but `addr_in` treats them as split.
2. `crates/mortar_core/src/layout.rs:919` (`addr_in`): follows from 1.
3. `crates/mortar_layout/src/symbols.rs:233`: likewise.
4. `crates/mortar_merge/src/order.rs:92`: `offsets` sorts all live fragments, so S-29's pass costs about 25 ms, not 2 to 3. `FragId` order within a merge class is already correct: bucketing by (output section, class) and concatenating would avoid the sort.
5. S-100: the internal `.comment` piece needs the internal file's `SectionId` (model, input).
6. `phase::run` over `Tables` (the pipeline's `Inputs`, `Resolution`, `Assignment`) is exercised by no test. Every test goes through the bare-object `Link`. An integration test needs `mortar_input` and `mortar_resolve` as dev-dependencies.
7. When the store is staged, one owned buffer per non-resident `Merge` section is held for the whole phase. A representative-bytes arena (keep only the first occurrence's bytes) would bound this by the output size.
8. Equal-hash, unequal-bytes interning (S-28 "bytes decide") is not exercised: a 64-bit collision cannot be forced without a hash hook. `merge.rs` compares bytes on every hash match.
9. x86-64 and i386 relocation classes come from a test table (`tests/support/mod.rs`) until `mortar_x86` exists.
