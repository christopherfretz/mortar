## 1. The records

Grammar (`spec/plumb.md` §3.2; `<align>` is hex and a power of two):

```
chunk synthetic <name> <addr> <size>
common <name> <addr> <size> <align>
copy <name> <addr> <size> <align>
```

- **`chunk synthetic`** in an allocated non-TLS `NOBITS` section: a
  linker-made `NOBITS` chunk of size above 0 that holds no allocation is
  now listed (today `.pad.relro`, the RELRO padding). Before, the chunk
  loop skipped every `NOBITS` chunk, and coverage excused an unlisted
  `NOBITS` section.
- **`common`** (new; after the chunk records): each common symbol the
  linker allocated, at its address and size, with the alignment it was
  placed at.
- **`copy`** (gains `<align>`): the alignment of the copied definition
  (P-D11, D-42) that the copy was placed at.

The rules (§3.3):

- **M6** — a synthetic chunk inside an allocated, not thread-local
  `NOBITS` section is that whole section (name, `sh_addr`, `sh_size`);
  such a section of size above 0 holding no record of the map (input
  section, piece, common, copy or chunk) is a finding.
- **M7** — a `common` record names a symbol the inputs define only as
  common, once; its address and size are the output `.symtab`'s, inside
  an allocated `NOBITS` section; its alignment is the largest its
  definitions ask and its address a multiple of it. In a stripped output
  the address and size are not judged. Every such name has a record.
- **M8** — a `copy` record is a `COPY` record of the output (one per
  address), with its symbol's name and `.dynsym` size, P-D11's alignment,
  and an address a multiple of it; every `COPY` record has one. A static
  output has no `COPY` record, so any `copy` record there is M8.

## 2. The version decision: `mortar-map 2`

The records change what a missing line means. Under version 2 no
`common` record states that no common symbol was allocated, no chunk in
a `NOBITS` section states that the linker made none, and a `copy`
without `<align>` is malformed. A version-2 reader taking a version-1
map would judge a correct link by a fact that map never stated, so the
reader refuses version 1 by its number (`map/mortar.rs`; test case
`"mortar-map 1\nend\n"`). The incremental and dynamic records of earlier
packages could stay in version 1 because their absence claims nothing;
these cannot. Version mentions were moved in `spec/image.md`,
`spec/cli.md`, `docs/status/options.md`, `mortar_cli` `rows.rs`, the
crates' doc strings and `xtask/src/plumb.rs`. The references' map readers
(GNU ld, lld, mold) are unchanged: `tests/maprec.rs`
`s3_3_foreign_maps_have_no_allocation_records`.

## 3. The inference removed

- `crates/mortar_plumb/src/dead.rs` `items()`: the block that took
  common symbols from the inputs (`common_only` over the objects, unless
  the map listed a `Common` chunk) and their place from the output's
  `.symtab`, and the block that gave each copy the alignment of its
  address (`1 << trailing_zeros`, capped at 64). Both now read
  `map.commons` and `map.dynamic.copies` with their stated alignment.
- `dead.rs` `coverage()`: `if claim && !nobits` is now `if claim` — an
  unlisted `NOBITS` section of a mortar map is P-I1.
- §6.8 says so; §6.9 (`--equiv`) reads version-2 maps: the identity of
  an allocation now includes each common (`m:<name>`) and copy
  (`y:<name>`), and P-E2 compares the common records by name.

Kept, with its reason: the after-common padding relaxation in
`coverage()` (a gap after a common or copy may reach the section's own
alignment). Measured over the 791 mortar maps of a kept corpus run:
with it, 0 findings; without it, 12 maps have a P-I1 finding, each the
tail of a copy block that mortar aligns up to `.bss`'s alignment
(e.g. `dyn-shdr` x86-64: `copy lib_var 43c0 4 8`, gap `[0x43c4,
+0x1c)`). The records state each allocation, not the block's tail.

## 6. Mutation run over M6-M8

In WP-091's manner: each check of `src/maprec.rs` dropped, or its
comparison flipped, one at a time; `tests/maprec.rs`, `tests/dyn.rs` and
`tests/mutation.rs` run first, the whole `mortar_plumb` suite for a
survivor. **28 mutants, 27 caught, 1 survivor.**

| rule | mutants | caught |
|---|---|---|
| M6 (required clause, chunk name/addr/size, TLS scope) | 5 | 4 |
| M7 (`common_only` max align and defined-name exclusion, required clause, second record, not common-only, align, addr a multiple, stripped skip, not in `.symtab`, undefined/special `st_shndx`, address and size compares, held in `NOBITS`: the check, its type test, its end bound) | 16 | 16 |
| M8 (required clause, second record, no `COPY` record incl. a static output, name, size, align, addr a multiple) | 7 | 7 |

The first run caught 17 of 28: no test left a record out, and none
changed the output's symbol under a record. Added for them:
`tests/maprec.rs` leaves out the padding chunk and each common record,
gives `b.o` a common `dat` that `a.o` defines in `.data` (not
common-only), and patches the output's `.symtab` entry of a common
(local; `SHN_ABS`; moved into `.data`; sized past `.bss`);
`tests/dyn.rs` leaves out a copy record and states an alignment past
the address's.

The survivor: dropping the required clause's exemption of thread-local
`NOBITS` sections. It makes the rule stricter, not weaker, and differs
only on a `.tbss` that holds no record of the map. No test link has
one; this was not measured on the corpus. The exemption stays because
`.tbss` addresses are offsets in the TLS template, not memory the
section owns.
