# WP-109b — the verifier reads its inputs by position

Branch `wp/109b-positioned-reads`, cut from afbdb15. Only
`crates/mortar_plumb/`, `xtask/src/plumb.rs`, `spec/plumb.md` and this
record. The design is WP-109 §6; no linker crate was read. Measured as in
WP-109 (the AArch64 development container, the dev-profile verifier as
the bench and CI run it, every large run under `prlimit
--as=8589934592`, `nice -n 10`, `ulimit -c 0`, one at a time, load under
10 and 9 GB or more available).

## 1. The steps

| step | commit | what |
|---|---|---|
| 1. the interface | 4fc2cdf | `mortar_plumb::Source` (`count`, `path`, `len`, `read_at`); `verify_from`; xtask's `FileSource` (the command's open files) |
| 2. skeletons resident | dc8b58a | `store.rs`: per object the skeleton, bodies read by position through a 64 MiB section cache, the last two objects whole when a phase loads them |
| 3. rules after the engine by position | (in dc8b58a) | see below |
| 4. `--equiv` over a Source | e1fe3ea | one store for both sides; the trace without a per-place map; offsets in 32 bits |
| §8.1 | 2c2bd5c | what is held, the measurements, what `--equiv` costs |
| merge | aed84da | `int/batch-3` (origin/main had not moved from afbdb15) |

**Step 3 has no commit of its own.** Step 2 changed every site that read
a section's bytes (`Obj::data(si)` returning a `Bytes` that is either a
slice of the skeleton or a body read on demand), the phases after the
engine included: content, erratum, `.gdb_index` names, properties,
eh_frame, the x86 and x86-64 record windows, ODR tables (which read only
`.mason.meta`, always in the skeleton). So step 2's tree already met
step 3's targets (below), and there was nothing left for step 3 to move.

**A deviation from WP-109 §6.3.** §6.3 has the caller list the inputs
(`inputs()`, members included). The `Source` names files only, by path;
the verifier finds a regular archive's members itself from the member
headers it reads by position (`store.rs`, `archive::header_span`). A thin
archive's listing, and any archive the by-position reader does not
accept, is read whole and parsed by `mortar_elf` as before. Reason: the
member layout is what the map names, and the verifier already parses
archives; asking the caller to do it would be a second parser to trust.

## 5. The follow-up: the outputs by position (design, for a package)

**What.** `--equiv` takes the two outputs through a `Source` too. Each
output is held whole only while its own side's engine runs (the engine
reads the output at every record); between and after, it is read by
position. The comparison phases read output bytes at eight sites in
`equiv.rs` (`section_data`, `value`, `bytes`, `nonalloc_bytes`, used by
the record, table, packed, rest and unit comparisons); each becomes a
positioned read of the span it compares.

**How.**

- `image.rs`: split `Image` into owned metadata (headers, sections,
  segments, symbols, dynamic) and a byte reader (`Bytes` over either the
  whole buffer or a Source). About 100 lines.
- `equiv.rs`: `check_from` takes the output sources; `run_side` loads
  its output whole, runs, drops it; the kept `Side` borrows nothing
  from the bytes; the eight sites read by position. About 150 lines.
- xtask `run_equiv`: open the two outputs as a `FileSource`. About 30
  lines.
- Tests: the comparisons equal on whole bytes and by position; an
  output that changed size between sides is a finding, not a panic.
  About 100 lines.
- Verdict replay by this package's method (every `--equiv` call in the
  link tests, old against new).

**Expected.** The peak becomes side F's engine without X's output: about
3.2 GiB on clang `-g` (2,506 - 1,163 + side X's 703 kept + F's engine),
and about 2.0 GiB at the comparisons. Under 4 GB with room. About one to
two days.

  `[ci] ok`. The links were not re-measured after the merge.
