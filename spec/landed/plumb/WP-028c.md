## S-17 piece lookup

The verifier found the piece of a section symbol's `st_value` and then
added A. `synth.md` S-17 requires the piece that holds byte `st_value +
A` (bias included), with S-18 (r < 0 or r ≥ `sh_size`) outside every
piece. Fixed in `crates/mortar_plumb/src/reloc.rs` (`resolve_at`,
`section_piece`; S-18 is `Target::Outside`, R6 under mortar's map).
Named symbols keep S-19: the piece of `st_value`, A after it, the
end-of-piece case only at `v == sh_size` (half-open `in_section`, and
`symbols.rs`). Every piece lookup now goes through one of the two:
`record`, the tombstone check (§5.8), `tls_target`, `eh.rs`'s pointers.
Regression test: `tests/pieces.rs` on a new corpus case `pieces`, which
is R1 without the fix. Acceptance: after `git merge main`, the WP-027b
filter in `crates/mortar_image/tests/link.rs` is removed and the test
asserts `report.findings.is_empty()`; it passes.

  X1–X4 and the TLS forms (IE, TLSDESC, GD, LD, large-model GD/LD) per

  map's linker writes (mortar's forms under mortar's map). New rewrite
  ids `got-to-lea`, `got-to-direct`, `got-to-imm`, `got-to-mov-imm`. A
  record that cannot be judged (stripped output, unusable `PT_TLS`)
  exempts its rewrite window from S12. `eh.rs` (pointer types) and
  `debug.rs` (`64` as ABS64) are per machine.
- **2. TLS variant II.** X-46 per linker and section kind; X-53 (`64`

  is S + A − GOT); undefined weak T per linker (X-18);
  `_TLS_MODULE_BASE_` descriptor T (X-47) and Y5's value (GNU ld's is
  align_up(p_memsz, p_align), at TP).
- **6. L3 judges x86-64** (`xtask/src/linktest/engine.rs` `not_judged`):
  297 PASS, 0 FAIL, 3 XFAIL (mold commons) on the full run; i386 outputs

- **3. IFUNC on x86-64** (§4.8, F5, `src/ifunc.rs` `x64_iplt`): IBT
  outputs' IPLT entries start with `endbr64`; mortar's are exactly X-37
  or X-38; each IFUNC word holds the linker's initial value (X-39). The
  `.got.plt` base is `_GLOBAL_OFFSET_TABLE_`; `__rela_iplt_*` (F1) as on
  AArch64.
- **7. Mutations** (§9.2 (m)): below.
- **4. x86 properties per X-41** (§6.6, `src/property.rs`): `And`
  0xc0000002..0xc0007fff, `Or` 0xc0000001 and 0xc0008000..0xc000ffff,
  `OrAll` 0xc0000000 and 0xc0010000..0xc0017fff; per-linker treatment

- **5. Layout.** S19 (§6.2): `.init`/`.fini` padding between fragments

## Reference-linker observations (§10.1, §10.2)

- New anomalies: A12 (lld, W4), A13 (mold, 4-aligned notes in an
  8-aligned `PT_NOTE`), A14 (lld, W9), A15 (GNU ld and mold, W10), A16
  (lld and mold, W8), A17 (mold, W6).
- mold relaxes a symbol's IE `mov` references to `mov $T` unless one of
  its references is an `add`; then every reference keeps the GOT slot.
- GNU ld gives an IFUNC loaded through the GOT only a `.got` slot with
  an `IRELATIVE` and no IPLT entry; its word is 0.
- lld's W4 write also reaches the last byte of a 16-byte GD sequence
  just before the 22-byte one.

## Spec errors

- **`spec/x64.md` X-47** (line 937): "Defined by the linker when
  referenced, hidden, at `tls.start`." GNU ld 2.44 gives it
  align_up(p_memsz, p_align), the TP offset of the block end, in a
  static x86-64 output (`tests/x64/tls.c` with `-mtls-dialect=gnu2`,
  the `tls-desc` case: Y5 holds with that value, and is a finding with
  `tls.start`'s 0).
- **`spec/x64.md` X-39** (line 823) and the §14.1 row "IFUNC word
  initial value": "GNU ld writes the word as the entry's address + 6".
  With an IBT IPLT (X-38 entries; `tests/x64/iplt-ibt.s`, the `w6` case,
  with or without `-z ibt`) GNU ld writes the entry's address itself
  (0x401000, 0x401010); a `.got` slot no entry loads (`movq
  ifn@GOTPCREL`) is 0.
- **`spec/x64.md` line 738 and the §14.1 row "`GOTTPOFF` IE → LE"**:
  "mold ... `GOTTPOFF` unrelaxed (IE kept)". mold 2.37.1 relaxes `mov`
  references to `mov $T` (I1's bytes) for a symbol none of whose
  references is an `add` (the `tls-ie` and `relax` cases; with an `add`
  to the same symbol in the object, both keep the GOT slot).
- **`spec/x64.md` §14.1 row "unknown x86 property"**: the GNU column
  says "warning"; GNU ld also drops the property (as X-41's table says).
  Experiment: two objects with 0xc0018000 (and a generic 0x7000):
  `readelf -n` shows neither in the output.
