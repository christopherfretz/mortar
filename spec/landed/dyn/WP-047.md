## The demand cells as implemented

These cells are for an alloc place in `Exec` or `Pie`. A non-alloc place
keeps the static rule. `TX` means a symbolic or RELATIVE record when the
place is writable, and `text-relocation` when it is read-only.

| class | local | abs | shlib data | shlib func | ifunc | weak0 | weakdyn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `AbsWord` exec | — | — | writable: EXPORT + Symbolic; read-only: COPY | writable: EXPORT + Symbolic; read-only: CANON_PLT | IPLT | — | writable: EXPORT + Symbolic; read-only: E |
| `AbsWord` pie | TX Relative | — | TX Symbolic | TX Symbolic | TX IPLT + Relative | — | TX Symbolic |
| `AbsNarrow` exec | — | — | COPY | CANON_PLT | IPLT | — | E |
| `AbsNarrow` pie | E | — | E | **`canonical-plt-in-pie`** (D-44, F-6) | E | — | E |
| `Pc`/`Lo` exec | — | — (`Pc`), — (`Lo`) | COPY | CANON_PLT | IPLT | — | E |
| `Pc`/`Lo` pie | — | E (`Pc`), — (`Lo`) | COPY (D-43) | **`canonical-plt-in-pie`** (D-44) | IPLT | — | E |
| `Branch` | — | — exec, E pie | PLT | PLT | IPLT | — (`X = 4`) | PLT |
| `Got` | GOT | GOT | GOT | GOT | GOT + IPLT | GOT | GOT |
| `GotRel` | GOT_BASE | GOT_BASE exec, E pie | E | E | IPLT + GOT_BASE | E | E |

- `E` is `relocation-incompatible`.
- A read-only `TX` place is `text-relocation` (D-47).
- The table above is what `arch.scan` and the scan driver give
  together. The `text-relocation` cells come from the driver's mapping
  of `ReadOnly` in an output with `.dynamic` (static PIE excluded).
- `weak-undefined-pc-in-pie` is per key (D-25), so `mortar_scan` raises
  it from `weak_use`'s classes, not mortar_a64.
- The test `s5_demand_cells_exec_and_pie` checks every cell above for
  every non-TLS M1+M2 type, against both writabilities and an independent
  transcription of §5. That is 1344 cells, with each refusal's name.
- The test `s8_tls_cells_exec_and_pie` checks the TLS classes against
  `local` and `shlib` TLS.

## Oracle

`tests/dynamic_ref.rs` uses hand-written assembly: four calls, TLSDESC,
IE and traditional GD against a shared object's TLS and the executable's
own, and an optional `.variant_pcs` reference. It is linked by GNU ld
2.44 and lld 19 over every combination of:

- {BTI property, none}
- {`pie`, `-no-pie`}
- {lazy, `-z now`}
- {without, with VPCS}

That is 32 links.

## Findings

- **F-1 (a64.md §8.1/§8.2, large TLSDESC).** §8.2 keeps the large form
  against a TLS-dynamic target as a descriptor. §8.1, however, rewrites
  each member "without looking at the others", and `TLSDESC_CALL` (569)
  is the same record in the small and the large form. Read literally, a
  kept large sequence gets its `blr x1` replaced by a `nop`, which breaks
  the program (`tls-desc-large` would read garbage).
  - mortar_a64 tells the forms apart by the record before the call:
    `relax::large_call`. A 569 whose previous record is a 568 against the
    same key, on the word before, is the large form's. It keeps its
    `blr` and demands `TLS_DESC`.
  - Scan refuses by name a kept `TLSDESC_ADD` that is not directly
    followed by its `CALL`: `Refused{Feature, "large-model TLSDESC whose
    TLSDESC_ADD is not followed by its TLSDESC_CALL"}`
    (`LARGE_TLSDESC_DYNAMIC`, `src/apply.rs`). No kept large `CALL` can
    therefore be missed.
  - `tls-desc-large`'s `desc.S` has this order (`ldr`, `add`, `blr`).
  - Proposed §8.2 sentence, after "stays a descriptor": "Its
    `TLSDESC_CALL` is the record right after the sequence's
    `TLSDESC_ADD` (same symbol, next word) and is not rewritten; a kept
    `TLSDESC_ADD` without such a `TLSDESC_CALL` is refused by name."
  - The fill of 569 now reads one neighbouring record. That is a
    sequence in arch.md §3.5's sense, within one relocation section, the
    same as R4. `Facts.seq_leads` is unchanged, because `leads = true` is
    always correct and `large_call` does not consult it.
  - **R1l against GNU ld:** for the executable's own TLS, GNU ld writes
    `T` as three words (`movz x0, #T[63:48], lsl #48`, `movk` 32, `movk`
    0) plus two `nop`s. §8.1 writes two words plus three `nop`s. x0 ends
    equal: 8 links compared by value. This is a recorded difference, not
    a defect.
- **F-6 (D-44/D-47, precedence; after the merge of WP-046).** The rule
  applied: in a `Pie`, a use whose `Exec` cell against
  `Shlib{func: true}` is `C` and that has no dynamic record of its own
  (`Pc`, `Lo`, `AbsNarrow`) is `canonical-plt-in-pie`. That is decided
  before its width and before the place's writability; only `AbsWord`,
  which has its own symbolic record in a `Pie`, falls to D-47's
  `text-relocation` in a read-only place.
  - `mortar_arch::class` (WP-046) already answers this way.
    `s5_demand_cells_exec_and_pie` had `E` for `AbsNarrow`, and the
    test was what was wrong. It is fixed; `mortar_arch` is unchanged.
  - Measured (AArch64 `.rodata` word against a shared function, PIE):
    - GNU ld refuses `ABS32`, `ABS16` and `PREL32` with "can not be used
      when making a shared object" (`PREL32`: "…; recompile with -fPIC").
      These are D-44's refusal texts, and there is no width message.
    - lld links `ABS32` and `PREL32` through a canonical PLT (W11's
      kind). It refuses `ABS16` only because the canonical PLT's address
      overflows 16 bits: "relocation R_AARCH64_ABS16 out of range". That
      is downstream of the canonical PLT, so it is not a competing name.
    - `PREL32`/`PREL16` are `Pc` on AArch64 and already took this path.
  - x86-64 answers the same:
    - `mortar_x64` calls the same `class_outcome` with no override, so
      `R_X86_64_32`/`32S` (`AbsNarrow`) give `canonical-plt-in-pie` too.
    - GNU ld refuses `R_X86_64_32` against a shared function in a PIE
      with "can not be used when making a PIE object; recompile with
      -fPIE".
    - lld links it, which is W11.
  - Proposed D-44 sentence: "In `Pie` the refusal covers every class
    whose `Exec` cell against a shared function is `C` except `AbsWord`
    (`Pc`, `Lo`, `AbsNarrow`), before any range check; a read-only
    `AbsWord` is `text-relocation` (D-47)."
  - After the merge, mortar_a64's `dynamic_outcome` was removed. The
    reason was a second failure, `mortar_scan` `d47_text_relocation`:
    `dynamic_outcome` turned `ReadOnly` into `text-relocation` in every
    `Pie`, static PIE included, because the architecture cannot tell the
    two apart. WP-046's driver already makes that mapping, gated on
    `.dynamic` (`mortar_scan/src/driver.rs` `dynamic_error`), and its
    `class.rs` carries D-43/D-44. mortar_a64 now reports the generic
    outcome, and I-4 is done.
- **F-2 (a64.md §10.3, BTI entries).** lld 19 leaves out `bti c` in a PLT
  entry whose address is not taken, and ends the entry with a second
  `nop`. GNU ld keeps it. mortar keeps it, because a range thunk reaches
  the entry with `br x16` (§9).
  - Proposed note in §10.3: "lld writes the landing pad only in entries
    whose address is taken; mortar writes it in every entry, as GNU ld
    does."
- **F-3 (`spec/dyn.md` D-30 row 25, `VariantPcsJumpSlot`).** GNU ld sets
  `DT_AARCH64_VARIANT_PCS` when only the executable's *reference* carries
  `STO_AARCH64_VARIANT_PCS`. lld sets it only from the shared object's
  *definition*. Both set it when the definition is marked.
  - D-30 should say whose `st_other` counts.
  - Proposed text: "…when a `JUMP_SLOT`'s symbol is marked
    `STO_AARCH64_VARIANT_PCS` in any input that references or defines it
    (GNU ld's; lld reads the definition only)."
- **F-4 (§7.1, type 256).** The withdrawn `R_AARCH64_NONE` (256) is not
  in §7.1, so per D-25 it is unfillable. It should probably be "no use of
  the key", as 0 is.
  - Proposed text: add 256 to the `NONE` row.
  - Implemented as written (unfillable) until ruled.
- **F-5 (`BTI_PLT` in static-pie).** GNU ld and lld write
  `DT_AARCH64_BTI_PLT` in a static-pie and in an output with no PLT
  entries, whenever the property is set. `TagWhen::BtiProperty` already
  says that. This note is for whoever evaluates the condition.
- **WP-051 (a), W15.** GNU ld relaxes GD without checking the `nop`.
  Mortar keeps §8.4's check (`relax::gd_lead`), so the case
  `tls-gd-unrelaxed` keeps the pair (R-A5, `TLS_GD`). This is tested in
  `d48_tls_against_a_shared_definition`.
- **WP-051 §4 item 4 (b).** The corrected sentence for a64 §8.2 is "GNU
  ld relaxes the large form too (to IE against a shared definition, LE
  against its own); lld and mold do not know 565-568; mortar keeps the
  descriptor against a shared definition (F-1)". mortar's behaviour
  follows §8.2.
- **WP-051 §4 item 12 (the `weak0` ADRP).** This is answered by the
  supervisor's ruling below. In every output kind, mortar writes §7's
  `X = Page(P + A) − Page(P)`, the instruction's page, as GNU ld and lld
  do.
- **WP-051 §4 item 11 (`VARIANT_PCS` tag order).** mortar_a64 lists
  `BTI_PLT`, then `VARIANT_PCS`, as D-30 rows 24-25 do. See also F-3.
- **WP-051 (d).** `variant-pcs-plt` is AArch64-only; see F-3 for the
  condition.
- **The supervisor's `weak0` ruling.** `ADRP`/`ADD` against `weak0` in
  `exec` give §7's bytes, with no refusal (`s7_branches_and_weak0_in_dynamic_outputs`).
  §7 and D-25's table were not found to disagree on a byte.

## Interface requests

- **I-1 (`mortar_arch::SectionOut`).** Add `pub writable: bool`, or have
  the image's record writer put `DynRole::place` content over the place.
  - Why: in `Exec`, a key with COPY or CANON_PLT, because of a read-only
    use, can also have a writable `ABS64` use. That use counts a Symbolic
    record and its place should hold 0 (D-46). Fill cannot tell the two
    places apart, so it writes `S + A` (the copy's or the entry's
    address).
  - Effect: the value at run time is the same, but the bytes break D-46.
    Documented at `src/apply.rs` `symbolic_place`.
- **I-2 (`mortar_scan` `WeakUses`, WP-046).** D-25's thread-local weak
  refusal in `Exec`/`Pie` (`Refused{Feature, "thread-local undefined weak
  symbol in a dynamic executable"}`, once per key) needs a TLS bit per
  use. `weak_use` returns `Unfillable` for every TLS type, which is the
  same class as a non-TLS unfillable use.
  - Proposal: `WeakUses` records `howto.class.is_tls()` beside the class.
  - mortar_a64 cannot raise the refusal itself, for two reasons: it is
    per key, and `OutputKind::Pie` includes static-pie, where §7 accepts
    the case.
- **I-3 (the thunk planner).** `PatchCx.target_word` must carry the
  synthetic PLT entry's first word when a thunk targets a PLT entry.
  Otherwise a BTI output refuses the thunk with `BTI landing pad`.
- **I-4**: done by WP-046 (see F-6).
